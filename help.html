The app for your TV

On an Android TV box (like the NVIDIA Shield), phone or tablet, use TunaReceive, the companion app made for TunaStream. Download the APK ending in arm64-v8a from TunaReceive's releases, send it to the device (LocalSend works well), and open it there to install. After that it updates itself.

On other devices, Moonlight works with TunaStream too.

Pairing a device

  1. Put the device on the same network as this PC. Guest Wi-Fi networks usually block this.
  2. Open TunaReceive. This PC appears by itself; if it doesn't, choose Add PC Manually and type the address shown on TunaStream's home page.
  3. Choose the PC. The device shows a four-digit PIN.
  4. Here, open the PIN page, type the PIN, name the device and press Send.

Paired devices are remembered, including after reinstalling TunaStream.

The picture stutters

On a connection that shows no dropped packets, stutter usually means the stream asks for slightly more than the Wi-Fi can carry at busy moments. Leave room:

  • In TunaReceive, open the PC's menu (hold the select button on it) and choose Fit picture quality to this connection. It measures the connection and suggests a picture quality with headroom.
  • Connect this PC to the router with a cable if you can. That helps more than anything else.
  • Put the TV box on 5 GHz Wi-Fi, or a cable.
  • The home page shows the stream's health while someone is playing, and says whether the network, this PC or the device is holding it back. With TunaReceive, it also shows what the TV sees.
  • If the home page says This PC is on Wi-Fi, every frame crosses the air twice. A cable from this PC to the router is the biggest single improvement.

What adjusts itself

With TunaReceive, the stream fits itself to the connection while you play, so a busy moment softens the picture instead of stuttering:

  • Picture quality steps down a little as soon as frames start arriving slowly, before any are lost, and further if they are lost. It steps back up once the connection clears.
  • Picture size follows when the quality has dropped a long way: a 4K stream is sent at 1440p or 1080p and the TV stretches it to fill the screen, then goes back to 4K. The change happens between two frames, without a pause.
  • Packet-loss protection uses less on a clean connection and more on a rough one.

Afterwards, TunaReceive can tell you how the stream went: hold the select button on the PC and choose How the last stream went. If quality was turned down for most of the game, a lower Picture quality setting gives a steadier picture.

The TV adds delay

TVs process the picture to make films look better, which adds delay that you feel in a game. TunaReceive asks the TV for its game mode while a game is on screen. This needs a TV with auto low latency mode (ALLM), turned on in the TV's HDMI settings. If the TV doesn't switch by itself, choose its Game mode by hand.

The controller doesn't rumble

Find out which half is broken first. Start a stream, then press Rumble now on the Troubleshooting page.

The controller buzzed

TunaStream, the network and the app are fine; the game isn't sending rumble.

  • Under Settings, set the controller type the PC sees to Xbox 360. Most PC games only rumble an Xbox controller.
  • Check the game's own vibration setting, and Steam Input for that game.

Nothing happened

  • Check that Rumble is on in TunaReceive's settings.
  • A controller connected over Bluetooth only rumbles if the TV box supports rumble for it. Try it with a cable, or in another app on the same device.

8BitDo Ultimate 2 Wireless

  • On a Shield or other Android TV box, connect it over Bluetooth in D mode. The 2.4G adapter that comes with it is meant for Windows PCs; plugged into a TV box it is seen but doesn't control anything.
  • Over Bluetooth, some Android TV boxes don't pass rumble to 8BitDo controllers. The Rumble now test shows whether yours does.
  • Update the controller's firmware with 8BitDo Ultimate Software on a PC: connection and rumble fixes arrive that way.
  • Back paddles reach the PC only as the buttons they're mapped to.

The game opens on the PC's monitor

Each stream gets its own screen on this PC, sized for the TV, and TunaStream makes it the main display while someone is playing. Programs that remember their monitor (Steam's Big Picture, some games) can still open on your monitor, so for three minutes after a game starts TunaStream moves its new windows onto the stream's screen (for as long as it runs, in Shared PC mode). Windows you open yourself stay where they are.

  • If the TV shows a blank screen and the home page says the virtual screen has a problem, press Repair virtual screen on the Troubleshooting page.
  • The log shows each window it moved: Moved a window onto the stream's screen.

Using the PC while someone streams

Turn on Shared PC under Settings > Shared PC. Your monitor stays the main screen, so your taskbar, windows and notifications stay with you; the streamed game's windows are kept on the TV's screen; only the game's sound goes to the stream, while yours stays on your speakers; and your mouse can't wander onto the TV's screen.

  • Windows has one keyboard focus. When you click your own windows, most games keep running and keep reading the controller, but some pause. When the controller is in use and you've left your mouse and keyboard alone for 8 seconds, TunaStream gives the game focus back; anything you type or click comes first. A game's "pause in background" option, if it has one, avoids the pause altogether.
  • On by default. If a game insists on opening full screen on your monitor, set it to Windowed in its settings: TunaStream takes off the border and stretches it over the TV (Windowed games fill the TV). Or turn on Make the TV's screen the main one, so games open there by themselves.
  • Do not disturb during streams (on by default) keeps notifications from popping up over the game; they wait in the notification centre. It also quiets them for you at the PC until the stream ends. With the TV's screen as the main one, Windows' own windows (File Explorer, Settings, Task Manager, dialogs) move to your screen like any program, but the taskbar's clock and the "Do you want to allow" prompt stay on the TV, as Windows only draws them on the main screen.
  • Under Devices, the Controller only preset keeps the TV's mouse and keyboard off your desktop.
  • The log says which program's sound was sent to the stream: Shared PC mode: ... sound goes to the stream.

Play time, messages and sounds

Set a daily limit for each device under Devices > Daily play time. The TV is warned at 10, 5 and 1 minute left, then the game closes, and that device can't start another stream until tomorrow. +15 min on the home page or the phone page gives more time today.

While someone plays, the home page (and the phone page, from the QR code) can show a message on the TV or play a sound. Add your own sounds with Add a sound: OGG, MP3 or WAV, a few seconds long.

HDR looks washed out, too dark or too bright

Games pick their HDR brightness from what Windows says the screen can show. The stream's screen is a virtual one, so Windows doesn't know your TV's real limits and reports a very bright screen; games then aim for highlights the TV can't show. TunaReceive tells TunaStream how bright your TV says it can go, and the home page shows it under Playing now.

To fix it, calibrate the stream's screen once with Windows' own tool:

  1. Install Windows HDR Calibration from the Microsoft Store (free, Windows 11).
  2. Start a stream with HDR on, from the device you calibrate for.
  3. Open Windows HDR Calibration on the stream's screen (it opens on the main display; with Shared PC on, drag it to the TV's screen first).
  4. Follow its three test patterns with the controller, looking at the TV.

Windows keeps the result for that screen, and the stream's screen keeps the same identity for each device, so it lasts. Games then aim for your TV's real brightness.

Stopping and starting

What happenedWhat TunaStream does
You chose End the session in the app, or pressed End session on the home page Closes the game, removes the stream's screen, and your monitor is the main display again.
You left the stream without ending it Keeps the game running so you can carry on, from the TV or from this PC (the home page has a button for that).
The connection dropped Keeps the game running so the device can reconnect.

To always close the game when the last device leaves, turn that on in Settings.
